# M1 results: physics engine vs three real pitchers

> **Note, 2026-11-04:** M1's spin-efficiency lower bound went from 0.20 to 0.01, and Mexico City (venue 5340, 7,349 ft) was added to the elevation table. Numbers here for pitch types that sat on 0.20 are from the old bound, and were not rerun. See `results/M3.md`.

**Short version:** with Cd and spin efficiency fitted per pitch type, the simulator lands
four-seam fastballs **2.03–4.02 in** from where they really crossed the plate (median, held-out
games). Every other pitch type (sinkers, cutters, breaking balls, offspeed) misses by
**2.92–9.14 in**, except Ohtani's curveball (1.79 in). Each type tends to miss in one
consistent direction.

- **Release start made every median worse**, by about the amount you'd expect from the
  extra flight time.
- **Per-game air density made most medians slightly better.**

The remaining misses are the M3 problem: movement that points somewhere other than where
`spin_axis` says.

## What was measured

- **Data:** Statcast, regular season only. Ohtani 2025 (753 pitches, 14 games), Sale 2026
  (2,544 usable of 2,575), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 usable of 2,649).
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for calibration, 30% held out (seed 0). Pitches
  from one game share air and mound, so a pitch-level split would leak.
- **Calibration:** per pitch type, fit Cd (drag) and spin efficiency (the share of spin that moves
  the ball) on training games, capped at 400 pitches (`n_train`). Then simulate each held-out
  pitch (`n_test`) to the front of the plate and measure the miss.
- **Target:** where Statcast's own fitted trajectory crosses the front of the plate (y = 17/12 ft).
  We don't use `plate_x/plate_z` directly, because their reference point moved in 2026 (see
  Caveats).
- **Error** is the distance in the x–z plane between the simulated and real crossing, in inches.
  **Median** is the typical miss; **p90** is a bad-but-not-worst miss. **Bias x/z** is the average
  signed miss (x +: toward the catcher's right; z +: too high). **Plate speed error** is the median
  |simulated − Statcast-fit| speed at the plate.
- **Headline variant `release+game`:** start at the release point, with each game's air density
  (venue elevation + game-time temperature).

### Release start made every row worse (`y50+const` vs `release+const`)

This is expected, and it isn't a reason to undo the change. The release start is the honest
setup, because M2 has to generate pitches from release conditions. The y = 50 ft start gets the
first few feet of flight for free from Statcast's fit, which already contains the real movement.

Why the error grows: if the spin force points slightly the wrong way, the miss grows like
acceleration × time², so a longer flight means a bigger miss. `validate.py` prints step [5] to
check this:

| | Flight-time ratio squared (T_release / T_y50)² | Observed error ratio, release+const / y50+const | Fit release speed − Statcast `release_speed` (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ohtani | 1.152–1.161 | 1.114–1.163 | −0.04 mph |
| Sale | 1.157–1.167 | 1.081–1.186 | −0.03 mph |
| Misiorowski | 1.119–1.131 | 1.111–1.135 | −0.18 mph |

The ranges run across pitch types; the per-type values are in the printed output. The error
grows by about as much as the extra flight time predicts. The fit's velocity, extrapolated back
to release, also agrees with Statcast's independently reported `release_speed` to within
0.18 mph. So the release state isn't an artifact of the extrapolation; the extra error is the
same direction problem with more time to grow.

### Per-game air helped a little, mostly (`release+const` vs `release+game`)

Every Sale and Misiorowski row improved. Ohtani's sweeper and sinker got slightly worse, and his
splitter didn't change. It's a real effect, but small next to the direction problem.

## Caveats

- **check [0]: Statcast moved its plate reference in 2026. This was a mismatch, not noise.**
  In 2025, the fit crosses `plate_x/plate_z` at the front edge of the plate (0.05 in at
  y = 17/12 ft). In 2026, it crosses them mid-plate (0.05 in at y = 0.708 ft for both Sale and
  Misiorowski; 0.68–0.95 in at 17/12).

  An earlier version of this write-up scored the simulator against `plate_x/plate_z` directly.
  That added a built-in error of up to about 1 in for the 2026 pitchers. Scoring now uses the
  fit's own crossing at 17/12 ft, which is the same in every season. Year and pitcher are
  confounded here (one 2025 pitcher, two 2026 pitchers), but the 0.05 in match at 0.708 ft is
  exact enough to call it a reference change.

  With the corrected target, Sale's changeup and sinker and three of Misiorowski's four types got
  better. Sale's fastball and slider and Misiorowski's fastball got worse: their old errors had
  been partly cancelled by the offset.
- **Release position equals `release_pos_*` by construction.** The start state is Statcast's
  fit evaluated at `release_pos_y`, and that fit is anchored on the reported release point. Their
  agreement is not independent evidence. Release *speed* is independent; see above.
- **Noise floor.** On *synthetic* data (fake pitches with known physics, not real results),
  `validate.py data/synthetic.csv --no-weather` still misses by 0.27–0.60 in (release+const). That
  is the cost of the constant-acceleration fit. Calibration there recovers Cd 0.341–0.349
  against a true 0.35. Treat anything under ~0.5 in as noise.
- **Roof/dome games use the reported temperature** (indoor temperature when the roof is closed).
  There's no humidity, wind or pressure.
- **`spin_axis`.** The common assumption is that public `spin_axis` is inferred from movement. If it
  were, check [1] would be close to 0°. Our data suggests otherwise:
  - The median |diff| is 6.5–19.3°.
  - The signed offset (printed per pitch type and saved in `spin_axis_offset.csv`) is consistent
    within most types (sliders scatter more) and differs between types.
  - Examples: Ohtani's sweeper +41.8° (std 12.7°), his sinker +22.3° and his split-finger +22.2°.
    Sale's sinker is −14.4° (std 5.6°); he's a lefty, so the direction flips. Misiorowski's
    four-seamer is only −3.5°.

  So the column carries information the movement doesn't, probably Hawk-Eye's measured axis.
  That isn't confirmed.
- **Spin efficiency here is a fit knob, not Savant's active-spin %.** It also absorbs any error in
  the lift formula. That's probably why four-seamers come out at 0.48–0.72 rather than the
  0.85–1.0 the README expected. Values of **0.20 sit on the fit's lower bound**, meaning the
  optimizer wanted ≤ 0.20: Ohtani's slider and cutter, and Misiorowski's cutter and slider.
- **Small held-out sets for Ohtani.** He threw only 14 regular-season games in 2025, so some
  held-out sets are tiny: sinker n_test = 5, curveball and splitter 7. Read his per-type numbers
  loosely.

## What's still wrong

The fit has one knob that scales how *much* the spin moves the ball, but nothing that changes
*which way* it moves. When real movement points away from `spin_axis`, the fit splits the
difference. That leaves a consistent signed miss, shown in the bias columns. The biggest ones:

- Ohtani's **sweeper** (bias z -7.63 in), **sinker** (x +5.25), **split-finger** (x +4.29, z +4.99),
  **slider** (x +4.50) and **cutter** (x -4.36).
- Sale's **sinker** (z +4.34) and **changeup** (z +2.71). His **fastball** (z -2.89) and **slider**
  (z -2.37) miss the other way.
- Misiorowski's **cutter** (x -3.50).

Sinkers, changeups/splitters and sweepers are the pitches known for seam-shifted wake: movement
caused by the seams' orientation rather than the spin. That's the M3 target. The gap between the
spin direction and the real movement is directly measurable, and M3 should model it. Gyro-heavy
cutters and sliders at the 0.20 efficiency bound point the same way: spin alone doesn't describe
them.

Fastballs are the cleanest case (2.03–4.02 in). Even so, all three pitchers' fastballs have a
negative z bias (they land too low). That fits the same direction mismatch better than a drag
problem. Plate speed error for fastballs is at most 0.62 mph.

## Reproduce

```bash
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Shohei Ohtani" --start 2025-03-01 --end 2025-11-30
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Chris Sale" --start 2026-03-01 --end 2026-11-30
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Jacob Misiorowski" --start 2026-03-01 --end 2026-11-30
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/660271_2025-03-01_2025-11-30.csv
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/519242_2026-03-01_2026-11-30.csv
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/694819_2026-03-01_2026-11-30.csv
```

The numbers above come from `results/m1/<pitcher>/summary.csv` (rounded to 2 decimals),
`spin_axis_offset.csv` and the script's printed checks ([0], [1], [5]). Each folder also has a
`report.png`.
