# M1 results: physics engine vs three real pitchers

**Short version:** with Cd and spin efficiency fitted per pitch type, the simulator lands
four-seam fastballs **1.99–4.02 in** from where they really crossed the plate (median, held-out
games). Every other pitch type (sinkers, cutters, breaking balls, offspeed) misses by
**3.06–9.12 in**, except Ohtani's curveball (1.82 in), and each type tends to miss in one
consistent direction. Starting from the release point made every median *worse*. Ohtani's sweeper
took the biggest hit, going from 7.64 to 8.89 in. Per-game air density made most medians
slightly *better*. The remaining misses are the M3 problem: movement
that points somewhere other than where `spin_axis` says.

## What was measured

- **Data:** Statcast, regular season only. Ohtani 2025 (753 pitches, 14 games), Sale 2026
  (2,544 usable of 2,575), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 usable of 2,649).
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for calibration, 30% held out (seed 0). Pitches
  from one game share air and mound, so a pitch-level split would leak.
- **Calibration:** per pitch type, fit Cd (drag) and spin efficiency (share of spin that moves the
  ball) on training games, capped at 400 pitches (`n_train`). Then simulate each held-out pitch
  (`n_test`) to the plate and measure the miss.
- **Error** = distance in the x–z plane between simulated and reported `plate_x/plate_z`, in
  inches. **Median** is the typical miss and **p90** is a bad-but-not-worst miss. **Bias x/z** is
  the average signed miss (x +: toward the catcher's right; z +: too high). **Plate speed error** is
  the median |simulated − Statcast-fit| speed at the plate.
- **Headline variant `release+game`:** start at the release point, with each game's air density
  (venue elevation + game-time temperature).

How to read it:

- **Release start made every row worse** (y50+const vs release+const). Most rows got a little
  worse, but Ohtani's sweeper went from 7.64 to 8.89 in. That isn't a reason to undo it.
  The release start is the honest setup, because M2 has to generate pitches from release
  conditions. The y = 50 ft start gets the first ~5 ft of flight free from Statcast's fit, which
  already contains the real movement. Our likely explanation: from release, the simulator has
  a few more feet of flight in which the wrong-direction spin force (below) builds up error.
- **Per-game air helped a little, mostly** (release+const vs release+game). Every Sale and Misiorowski
  row improved. Ohtani's sweeper and sinker got slightly worse and his splitter didn't change. It's a real effect, but
  small next to the direction problem.

## Caveats

- **Release position equals `release_pos_*` by construction.** The start state is Statcast's
  fit evaluated at `release_pos_y`, and that fit is anchored on the reported release point. Their
  agreement is not independent evidence.
- **Noise floor.** On *synthetic* data (fake pitches with known physics, not real results), the
  release-start pipeline still misses by a median 0.735 in, and calibration recovers Cd ≈ 0.34–0.35
  against a true 0.35. That miss is the cost of the constant-acceleration fit. Treat anything
  under ~0.5–0.75 in as noise.
- **2026 data check [0] is not ~0.** For Sale (0.95 in) and Misiorowski (0.68 in), Statcast's own
  fit doesn't land exactly on the reported plate location. For Ohtani 2025 it does (0.05 in).
  Year and pitcher are confounded here (one 2025 pitcher, two 2026 pitchers), so this is
  *possibly* a change in Statcast's 2026 data rather than something specific to these pitchers.
  Either way, it means up to ~0.7–0.95 in of extra noise floor (check [0]) for Sale and
  Misiorowski.
- **Roof/dome games use the reported temperature** (indoor temperature when the roof is closed).
  No humidity, wind or pressure.
- **`spin_axis`.** The common assumption is that public `spin_axis` is inferred from movement. If
  it were, check [1] would be close to 0°. Our data suggests otherwise: the median |diff| is
  6.5–19.3°, and the signed offset (printed per pitch type; also in `spin_axis_offset.csv`) is
  consistent within a type and differs between types. Examples: Ohtani's sweeper +41.8°
  (std 12.7°), sinker +22.3° and split-finger +22.2°. Sale's sinker is −14.4° (std 5.6°); he's a
  lefty, so the direction flips. Misiorowski's four-seamer is only −3.5°. So the column carries
  information the movement doesn't, probably Hawk-Eye's measured axis. That isn't confirmed. Either way, the simulator pushes
  the ball along `spin_axis`, and the real ball doesn't always go that way.
- **Spin efficiency here is a fit knob, not Savant's active-spin %.** It also absorbs any error in
  the lift formula, which is probably why four-seamers come out at 0.48–0.69 rather than the
  0.85–1.0 the README expected. Values of **0.20 sit on the fit's lower bound**, meaning the
  optimizer wanted ≤ 0.20: Ohtani's slider and cutter, Misiorowski's cutter and slider.
- **Small held-out sets for Ohtani.** He threw only 14 regular-season games in 2025, so some held-out
  sets are tiny: sinker n_test = 5, curveball and splitter 7. Read his per-type numbers loosely.

## What's still wrong

The fit has one knob that scales how *much* the spin moves the ball, but nothing that changes
*which way* it moves. When real movement points away from `spin_axis`, the fit splits the
difference. That leaves a consistent signed miss, the bias columns. The biggest ones:

- Ohtani's **sweeper** (bias z -7.64 in), **sinker** (x +5.29), **split-finger** (x +4.30, z +4.99),
  **cutter** (x -4.38) and **slider** (x +4.49).
- Sale's **sinker** (z +4.99) and **changeup** (z +3.57).
- Misiorowski's **cutter** (x -3.94).

Sinkers, changeups/splitters and sweepers are the pitches known for seam-shifted wake: movement
caused by the seams' orientation rather than the spin. That's the M3 target. The size of the gap
between the spin direction and the real movement is directly measurable, and M3 should model it.
Gyro-heavy cutters and sliders at the 0.20 efficiency bound point the same way: spin alone doesn't
describe them.

Fastballs are the cleanest case (1.99–4.02 in). Even so, all three have a negative z bias (too low),
which is consistent with the same direction mismatch rather than a drag problem. Plate speed error for
fastballs is at most 0.54 mph.

## Reproduce

```bash
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Shohei Ohtani" --start 2025-03-01 --end 2025-11-30
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Chris Sale" --start 2026-03-01 --end 2026-11-30
python scripts/fetch.py --name "Jacob Misiorowski" --start 2026-03-01 --end 2026-11-30
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/660271_2025-03-01_2025-11-30.csv
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/519242_2026-03-01_2026-11-30.csv
python scripts/validate.py data/raw/694819_2026-03-01_2026-11-30.csv
```

Numbers above come from `results/m1/<pitcher>/summary.csv` (rounded to 2 decimals),
`spin_axis_offset.csv` and the script's printed checks. Each folder also has a `report.png`.
