# M2 results: the per-pitcher generative model ("the arm") on three real pitchers

**Short version:** the model still does **not** pass the classifier two-sample test on real
data, but it is much closer than the first run (see "Why the evaluation rounds generated
pitches"). Of the 9 graded pitch-type rows (pitch types with at least 30 held-out pitches), **1
passes**: Sale's sinker (AUC 0.479, interval 0.354 to 0.603, from only 38 held-out pitches). The
other 8 fail, with Gaussian AUCs of **0.633 to 0.729**; fastballs are 0.667 to 0.698. A Gaussian
mixture, where BIC picked 2 components, helped by 0.011 to 0.077 AUC (best case Sale's
changeup, 0.670 to 0.593) but no mixture row reached 0.55. Conditioning on context (pitch
count, times through the order, count) raised held-out log-likelihood for most pitch types, by
small amounts, and hurt a few.

- **Nothing was tuned to make a pitch type pass.** The only model change in scope was
  Gaussian to mixture, and both are reported.
- **Movement is not graded here.** It is plotted for M3.
- Every number in the tables is copied from `results/m2/<pitcher>/summary.csv`, `ks.csv`,
  `context.csv` and `skipped.txt`.

## Why the evaluation rounds generated pitches

The first run of this milestone compared real pitches with *unrounded* generated ones, and the
fastball AUCs came out at 0.944 (Ohtani), 0.985 (Sale) and 0.997 (Misiorowski). The cause was
the decimals. Statcast publishes these columns rounded (in the raw CSV, Misiorowski's
four-seamer `release_extension` has only 14 distinct values over 1,677 pitches and `spin_axis`
27), while the generator draws continuous numbers, so a gradient-boosted classifier can win on
"is this a multiple of 0.1" alone. Ryan chose to fix the evaluation, not the generator: before
the AUC and KS comparisons, generated values are rounded to Statcast's reporting precision
(`release_pos_x/z` 0.01 ft, extension 0.1 ft, velocity 0.1 mph, spin rate 1 rpm, spin axis 1
degree, wrapped to [0, 360)). The two release angles are continuous on both sides and stay
unrounded. The generator, the plots and the flown movement stay unrounded. All numbers below
are from the rerun with rounding; nothing else changed.

## What was measured

- **Data:** the same three cached Statcast CSVs as M1, regular season. Ohtani 2025 (753 usable
  pitches), Sale 2026 (2,544 usable of 2,575), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 usable of 2,649). No
  pitch was missing a game temperature or venue.
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for fitting, 30% held out, seed 0 (same split
  function as M1). `n_train` and `n_test` below count pitches.
- **What is generated:** the joint distribution, per pitch type, of eight numbers at the hand.
  Six are graded: release side, release height, extension, velocity, spin rate, spin axis (the
  Hawk-Eye measured axis; the angle is handled by unwrapping around its circular mean). The
  other two are the vertical and horizontal release angles, kept only so M1 can fly a generated
  pitch; they are ungraded.
- **Models:** (1) a single multivariate Gaussian per pitch type; (2) a Gaussian mixture, 1 to 4
  components chosen by BIC on training games, tried only with at least 100 training pitches;
  (3) a context-conditioned Gaussian (mean linear in context, shared covariance), scored
  separately by log-likelihood.
- **Pass/fail (classifier two-sample test):** held-out real pitches vs an equal number of
  generated pitches (rounded like Statcast), six graded features, gradient-boosted classifier, 5-fold cross-validated
  AUC, averaged over 10 independent generated sets. **Pass means mean AUC <= 0.55.** 0.5 means
  the classifier cannot tell them apart. The bracket is a bootstrap 95% interval.
- **Ungraded rows:** pitch types with fewer than 30 held-out pitches are fitted and reported but
  not graded (Ohtani has several).
- **Marginals:** per graded feature, a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, real held-out vs
  generated (rounded the same way); flagged when p < 0.01. "KS-flagged" below lists the flagged features.
- **`auc8`:** the same AUC with the two release angles added. An ungraded diagnostic.

### What the table says

- **Every fastball and slider is above the bar, but only at 0.65 to 0.70.** Ohtani's fastball
  0.698, Sale's 0.678, Misiorowski's 0.667 (Gaussian); Sale's slider 0.654. The intervals sit
  well above 0.55 for the larger samples (for example Sale's fastball, 0.638 to 0.719).
- **KS flags are much sparser now.** Extension is flagged in 3 of the 9 graded rows (Ohtani FF,
  Misiorowski CU and FF), spin axis in 3 (Misiorowski CU, FC, SL), spin rate in 2 (Sale FF,
  Misiorowski FF), release height in 2 (Sale CH, SL), and velocity and release side in 1 each
  (Gaussian fits). Several rows have a single flagged feature. KS cannot say which feature
  the classifier is using.
- **The mixture helps in the rows where BIC chose 2 components.** Sale's changeup 0.670 to
  0.593, Sale's fastball 0.678 to 0.664, Misiorowski's fastball 0.667 to 0.656 and slider
  0.729 to 0.678. Rows where BIC kept 1 component are identical for both models. No mixture
  row reaches 0.55; Sale's changeup (0.593, interval 0.501 to 0.680) is the closest.
- **The one pass (Sale's sinker) is a small-sample pass.** Its interval, 0.354 to 0.603,
  includes 0.55 and reaches above it. It is also the only graded row with no KS-flagged feature.
  Read it as "not shown to differ", not "matches".
- **8-feature diagnostic:** adding the two release angles moves AUC by at most about 0.03 in
  either direction (for example Sale's sinker 0.479 to 0.512), so the angles are not what
  drives the failures.
- **Remaining gap, unexplained.** With rounding matched, the failures are moderate (0.63 to
  0.73) rather than near-perfect. I have not isolated what the classifier still uses. Candidates
  are the flagged features above, game-to-game drift between training and held-out games, and
  any other resolution difference (for example real values being rounded before Statcast
  derives other columns). None of these has been tested.

### What the context table says

- **Context helped in 9 of 14 rows, but modestly.** The biggest gains are the fastballs:
  Ohtani +0.366 and Misiorowski +0.236 (log-likelihood per pitch). Sale's fastball is +0.048
  and his slider +0.073. Ohtani's slider (+0.001) is a tie.
- **This test doesn't say which context feature matters.** It uses all four variables
  together.
- **It hurt in 5 rows:** Sale's changeup (-0.212) and sinker (-0.072), Misiorowski's cutter
  (-0.013), Ohtani's cutter (-0.043), and **Ohtani's sinker (-8.020)**. The sinker row has only
  48 training and 5 held-out pitches, and the per-pitch log-likelihoods of -54 and -62 look like
  an ill-conditioned fit on a tiny set rather than a real effect (my reading). Ignore it.
- The context numbers do not use the classifier, so rounding does not affect them (unchanged from
  the first run). A gain here only says the conditioned model fits held-out numbers better. It was not run
  through the classifier test (by design: it is not gated on the earlier models passing).

## Movement (ungraded, M3's job)

Each PNG has a "movement, not graded until M3" panel with three clouds: real pitches from
Statcast's own fit, real held-out pitches flown by M1, and generated pitches flown by M1. No
number is reported for it. Visual observation only, for two plots:

- **Misiorowski's four-seamer** (`results/m2/misiorowski-jacob/FF.png`): the generated cloud
  sits on top of the real M1-flown cloud and is tighter than the real Statcast cloud, which
  spreads wider and extends to smaller horizontal break (toward -6 in) than either M1 cloud.
  That gap is M1's job to close with M3, not the generator's.
- **Sale's sinker** (`results/m2/sale-chris/SI.png`): the generated and M1-flown real clouds
  overlap around 17 to 19 in horizontal and 6 to 9 in vertical break, while the real Statcast
  points run lower and further out (down to about 0 in vertical). It looks like the
  seam-shifted-wake gap M1 flagged for sinkers. One plot, so not a general statement.

## What's still wrong

1. **The test mostly fails.** 8 of 9 graded rows are above the 0.55 bar (0.633 to 0.729
   Gaussian; 0.593 to 0.678 for the mixtures where BIC chose 2 components). The milestone's
   done-condition (AUC close to 0.5) is not met.
2. **The remaining gap is unexplained** (see above). One thing is now settled: the first run's
   near-perfect AUCs were a rounding artifact, which is why the evaluation rounds generated
   pitches.
3. **Held-out games may differ from training games.** Some rows still flag spin rate, spin axis
   or extension, which could reflect game-to-game drift (mound, tracking calibration) that a
   fit pooled over training games cannot predict. Not investigated.
4. **Many Ohtani pitch types are too small to grade.** He threw 14 games in 2025, so most of
   his rows have 5 to 27 held-out pitches.
5. **No numbers for movement.** The generated pitches are flown, but the comparison waits for M3.
6. **The context model is only scored by log-likelihood.** It is not used for sampling in these
   runs and not tested by the classifier.
7. **Nothing is saved.** Models are refit on every run; M7 will need them stored.

## Future work

- **Find what the classifier still uses** (feature importance, per-game drift) now that
  rounding is matched.
- **Pool across pitchers for scale.** One pitcher's pitch type has a few hundred pitches. A
  model with structure shared across pitchers (for example, a mean shift per pitcher) would
  have far more data. Out of scope for M2 by design.
- **Save fitted models** so M7 can load them.
- **M3 seam effect** to close the movement gap seen in the PNGs.
- **Pick context features per pitch type** instead of all four at once.
