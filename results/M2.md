# M2 results: the per-pitcher generative model ("the arm") on three real pitchers

**Short version:** under the pass rule Ryan chose (the model's AUC must be within a noise-scaled
margin of the **real-vs-real floor**, see "The pass rule"), **6 of the 9 graded Gaussian rows
pass**: Ohtani FF, Sale FF and SI, Misiorowski CU, FC and FF. **3 fail**: Misiorowski's slider
(gap +0.130, margin 0.040), Sale's changeup (+0.046 vs 0.043) and Sale's slider (+0.021 vs
0.020). The last two miss by 0.003 and 0.001, so they are near misses, not clear failures. The
2-component mixture rescues Sale's changeup (gap -0.031) but not Misiorowski's slider (+0.079).
Under the old absolute bar (AUC <= 0.55, still reported as `pass_abs`) only 1 of 9 passes (Sale's
sinker), because held-out real pitches already score 0.599 to 0.681 against training-game real
pitches in 8 of 9 graded rows. Conditioning on context (pitch count, times through the order,
count) raised held-out log-likelihood for most pitch types, by small amounts, and hurt a few.

- **Nothing was tuned to make a pitch type pass.** The pass rule was changed once, by Ryan, after
  the earlier results were seen, to replace a bar the by-game split makes unreachable. The only
  model change in scope was Gaussian to mixture, and both are reported. The AUCs are identical to
  the previous run (same seed and code path); `auc_gap`, `auc_margin`, `pass`, `pass_abs` and
  `physical.csv` are new.
- **A pass means "not shown to differ from a perfect model of the training games"**, not "matches
  the pitcher". Small pitch types get wide margins (Sale's sinker 0.080) and so weak evidence.
- **Movement is not graded here.** It is plotted for M3.
- Every number below is copied from `results/m2/<pitcher>/summary.csv`, `ks.csv`, `context.csv`,
  `physical.csv` and `skipped.txt` (tables generated from the CSVs by a script, not typed).

History in brief: the first run used a fixed bar (AUC <= 0.55) and unrounded generated values,
giving fastball AUCs of 0.944 to 0.997 that came from Statcast's rounded decimals, not from the
physics. The evaluation now rounds generated pitches like Statcast does (release_pos_x/z 0.01 ft,
extension 0.1 ft, velocity 0.1 mph, spin rate 1 rpm, spin axis 1 degree, wrapped to [0, 360)); the
generator, plots and flown movement stay unrounded. Then the fixed bar proved unreachable under a
by-game split, hence the floor-relative rule.

## The pass rule (and why)

1. **The floor (`auc_floor`).** A model fitted on the training games can at best reproduce the
   training games. If held-out games differ from them (tracking calibration, mound, health,
   mechanics), a classifier tells training-game pitches from held-out pitches even with a perfect
   model. `auc_floor` measures exactly that: the same classifier, features and sample size, but
   real training-game pitches (as many as the held-out set) in place of generated ones. It is
   roughly the best AUC any model fitted on those games could reach. That is why a fixed 0.55 can't
   be met: the floor itself is above it in 8 of 9 graded rows.
2. **Pairing (`auc_gap`).** The test averages 10 generated sets (draws). For draw k, the model's
   AUC and the floor's AUC use the same held-out real pitches and the same cross-validation folds,
   so the luck of which held-out pitches were drawn cancels when subtracting:
   gap_k = AUC(model, k) - AUC(floor, k). `auc_gap` is the mean of the 10.
3. **The margin (`auc_margin`) scales with noise.** margin = max(0.02, 2 x sd(gap_k) / sqrt(10)),
   about two standard errors of the mean gap. A pitch type with few held-out pitches is noisy, so
   it gets a wide margin (Sale's sinker, 38 pitches: 0.080); one with hundreds is steady, so the
   margin is tight.
4. **Why a 0.02 minimum.** The 10 draws vary only the generated side; the held-out real set is the
   same in every draw, so the spread across draws misses part of that set's own noise. Without a
   minimum, a luckily small spread would give a tiny, unfair tolerance.
5. **`pass` = `auc_gap` <= `auc_margin`** on graded rows (pitch types with at least 30 held-out
   pitches). `pass_abs` is the old test (AUC <= 0.55), kept for reference only.

## What was measured

- **Data:** the same three cached Statcast CSVs as M1, regular season. Ohtani 2025 (753 usable
  pitches), Sale 2026 (2,544 usable of 2,575), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 usable of 2,649). No pitch
  was missing a game temperature or venue.
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for fitting, 30% held out, seed 0 (same split
  function as M1). `n_train` and `n_test` count pitches.
- **What is generated:** the joint distribution, per pitch type, of eight numbers at the hand. Six
  are graded: release side, release height, extension, velocity, spin rate, spin axis (the
  Hawk-Eye measured axis; unwrapped around its circular mean). The other two are the vertical and
  horizontal release angles, kept only so M1 can fly a generated pitch; they are ungraded.
- **Models:** (1) a single multivariate Gaussian per pitch type; (2) a Gaussian mixture, 1 to 4
  components chosen by BIC on training games, tried only with at least 100 training pitches; (3) a
  context-conditioned Gaussian (mean linear in context, shared covariance), scored separately by
  log-likelihood.
- **Classifier test:** held-out real pitches vs an equal number of generated pitches (rounded like
  Statcast), six graded features, gradient-boosted classifier, 5-fold cross-validated AUC, averaged
  over 10 independent generated sets. 0.5 means the classifier cannot tell them apart. The bracket
  is a bootstrap 95% interval for a single draw's AUC, averaged over the 10 draws; it is not an
  interval for the mean.
- **Ungraded rows:** pitch types with fewer than 30 held-out pitches are fitted and reported but
  not graded (Ohtani has several).
- **Marginals:** per graded feature, a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, real held-out vs 1,000
  generated (rounded the same way); flagged when p < 0.01. See `ks.csv`. KS is conservative on
  rounded, tied data and cannot say which feature the classifier uses.
- **Physical units (`physical.csv`, reported, not graded):** per graded feature, generated mean
  minus held-out real mean and the ratio of standard deviations, from the same 1,000 generated
  pitches as the KS test (rounded like Statcast).
- **`auc8`:** the same AUC with the two release angles added. An ungraded diagnostic.
- **Noise check:** a reviewer's null simulation (identical Gaussian on both sides, 20 repetitions,
  mean over 10 draws) gave an sd of the mean AUC of about 0.036 at n_test 38 and 72, and about 5%
  of repetitions above 0.55. That simulation was **not produced by any script in `scripts/`**.

**Ungraded rows, with a caveat on the numbers.** Ohtani's curveball (7 held-out) and sinker (5)

### What the table says

- **6 of 9 Gaussian rows pass** (Ohtani FF, Sale FF and SI, Misiorowski CU, FC, FF). Their gaps
  are -0.030 to +0.017, within margins of 0.020 to 0.080. Where the gap is about 0 (Sale FF +0.009,
  Misiorowski FC +0.007 and FF -0.004) the generated pitches are about as close to the held-out
  games as the training games themselves are.
- **Misiorowski's slider is the clear failure:** gap +0.130 against a margin of 0.040 (mixture
  +0.079 vs 0.043). Its floor is only 0.599 (interval 0.497 to 0.694) while the model is 0.729, so
  this one is not explained by drift. Only 62 held-out pitches, so both numbers are noisy.
- **Two near misses:** Sale's changeup (Gaussian +0.046 vs 0.043; the mixture, which BIC set to 2
  components, is -0.031 and passes) and Sale's slider (+0.021 vs 0.020, a 0.001 miss with the
  minimum margin binding). With a hard cutoff, differences this small should not be read as a real
  distinction between pass and fail.
- **The one `pass_abs` pass is Sale's sinker (0.479).** It also passes the new rule, with the
  widest margin in the table (0.080), because it has only 38 held-out pitches. With
  `min_samples_leaf=20` each training fold has about 61 samples, so the classifier has little power
  there. Read it as "not shown to differ".
- **Floors:** 8 of 9 graded floors are 0.599 to 0.681; Sale's sinker is 0.492 (n=38, interval 0.363
  to 0.625). So most of the old "fail" is plausibly game-to-game drift that no model fitted on
  other games can match. The floor does not say what the drift is.
- **The mixture helps only where BIC chose 2 components:** Sale CH 0.670 to 0.593, Sale FF 0.678 to
  0.664, Misiorowski FF 0.667 to 0.656, Misiorowski SL 0.729 to 0.678.
- **8-feature diagnostic:** adding the two release angles moves AUC by at most about 0.03 in either
  direction (for example Sale's sinker 0.479 to 0.512), so the angles are not what drives the
  failures.

## Movement (ungraded, M3's job)

Each PNG has a "movement, not graded until M3" panel with three clouds: real pitches from
Statcast's own fit, real held-out pitches flown by M1, and generated pitches flown by M1. No
number is reported for it. Visual observation only, for two plots:

- **Misiorowski's four-seamer** (`results/m2/misiorowski-jacob/FF.png`): the generated cloud
  sits on top of the real M1-flown cloud and is tighter than the real Statcast cloud, which
  spreads wider and extends to smaller horizontal break (toward -6 in) than either M1 cloud.
  That gap is M1's job to close with M3, not the generator's.
- **Sale's sinker** (`results/m2/sale-chris/SI.png`): the generated and M1-flown real clouds
  overlap around 17 to 19 in horizontal and 6 to 9 in vertical break, while the real Statcast
  points run lower and further out (down to about 0 in vertical). It looks like the
  seam-shifted-wake gap M1 flagged for sinkers. One plot, so not a general statement.

## What's still wrong

1. **Absolute AUC is far from 0.5.** Graded Gaussian AUCs are 0.479 to 0.729 and 8 of 9 are
   above 0.55. Under the floor-relative rule that is mostly held-out-game drift, but the
   milestone's literal "AUC close to 0.5" is not met, and the rule was changed after these
   numbers were seen (Ryan's call, written into the spec).
2. **Three Gaussian rows fail the floor-relative rule:** Misiorowski's slider (gap +0.130 vs
   margin 0.040, the clear model-side gap; mixture +0.079), Sale's changeup (+0.046 vs 0.043, a
   near miss; the mixture passes) and Sale's slider (+0.021 vs 0.020, a near miss). Causes are
   not investigated. Misiorowski's slider has only 62 held-out pitches.
3. **Drift is not modelled.** The floor shows it is there (0.599 to 0.681 in 8 of 9 graded
   rows), but nothing here fits it.
4. **Many Ohtani pitch types are too small to grade.** He threw 14 games in 2025, so most of
   his rows have 5 to 27 held-out pitches.
5. **No numbers for movement.** The generated pitches are flown, but the comparison waits for M3.
6. **The context model is only scored by log-likelihood.** It is not used for sampling in these
   runs and not tested by the classifier.
7. **Nothing is saved.** Models are refit on every run; M7 will need them stored.

## Future work

- **Model per-game drift** (for example a per-game random effect on release and spin means),
  since the floor says it dominates the remaining gap.
- **Set tolerances on the physical-units table** (reported, not graded) now that the numbers
  have been seen.
- **Find what the classifier still uses** (feature importance) now that rounding is matched.
- **Pool across pitchers for scale.** One pitcher's pitch type has a few hundred pitches. A
  model with structure shared across pitchers (for example, a mean shift per pitcher) would
  have far more data. Out of scope for M2 by design.
- **Save fitted models** so M7 can load them.
- **M3 seam effect** to close the movement gap seen in the PNGs.
- **Pick context features per pitch type** instead of all four at once.
