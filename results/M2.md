# M2 results: the per-pitcher generative model ("the arm") on three real pitchers

**Short version:** the model does **not** pass the classifier two-sample test on real data.
Of the 9 graded pitch-type rows (pitch types with at least 30 held-out pitches), **1 passes**:
Sale's sinker (AUC 0.534, but with a wide interval, 0.406 to 0.663, from only 38 held-out
pitches). The other 8 fail, most of them badly: fastballs and Sale's slider land at
**0.944 to 0.997** (a classifier tells real from generated almost perfectly), Misiorowski's
cutter at 0.944, and the other breaking balls and Sale's changeup at 0.76 to 0.86. A Gaussian
mixture, where BIC picked more than one component, helped only a little (best case
Misiorowski's slider, 0.764 to 0.734). Conditioning on context (pitch count, times through the
order, count) raised held-out log-likelihood for most pitch types, but by small amounts, and
hurt a few.

- **Nothing was tuned to make a pitch type pass.** The only model change in scope was
  Gaussian to mixture, and both are reported.
- **Movement is not graded here.** It is plotted for M3.
- Every number in the tables is copied from `results/m2/<pitcher>/summary.csv`, `ks.csv`,
  `context.csv` and `skipped.txt`.

## What was measured

- **Data:** the same three cached Statcast CSVs as M1, regular season. Ohtani 2025 (753 usable
  pitches), Sale 2026 (2,544 usable of 2,575), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 usable of 2,649). No
  pitch was missing a game temperature or venue.
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for fitting, 30% held out, seed 0 (same split
  function as M1). `n_train` and `n_test` below count pitches.
- **What is generated:** the joint distribution, per pitch type, of eight numbers at the hand.
  Six are graded: release side, release height, extension, velocity, spin rate, spin axis (the
  Hawk-Eye measured axis; the angle is handled by unwrapping around its circular mean). The
  other two are the vertical and horizontal release angles, kept only so M1 can fly a generated
  pitch; they are ungraded.
- **Models:** (1) a single multivariate Gaussian per pitch type; (2) a Gaussian mixture, 1 to 4
  components chosen by BIC on training games, tried only with at least 100 training pitches;
  (3) a context-conditioned Gaussian (mean linear in context, shared covariance), scored
  separately by log-likelihood.
- **Pass/fail (classifier two-sample test):** held-out real pitches vs an equal number of
  generated pitches, six graded features, gradient-boosted classifier, 5-fold cross-validated
  AUC, averaged over 10 independent generated sets. **Pass means mean AUC <= 0.55.** 0.5 means
  the classifier cannot tell them apart. The bracket is a bootstrap 95% interval.
- **Ungraded rows:** pitch types with fewer than 30 held-out pitches are fitted and reported but
  not graded (Ohtani has several).
- **Marginals:** per graded feature, a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, real held-out vs
  generated; flagged when p < 0.01. "KS-flagged" below lists the flagged features.
- **`auc8`:** the same AUC with the two release angles added. An ungraded diagnostic.

**Ungraded rows, with a caveat on the numbers.** Ohtani's curveball (7 held-out) and sinker (5)

exactly 0.500 with a zero-width interval, which looks like a degenerate result of a tiny test
set under 5-fold cross-validation rather than evidence the generated pitches are perfect (my
reading; not investigated). His sweeper (27 held-out) shows 0.715 [0.574, 0.845]. None of these
is graded.

### What the table says

- **Fastballs fail hardest, and they have the most data.** Ohtani 0.944, Sale 0.985,
  Misiorowski 0.997 (Gaussian). With more held-out pitches the classifier finds even small
  differences, so the large-sample rows are the least forgiving. Sale's slider (0.988) is as
  bad.
- **Extension is KS-flagged in 8 of the 9 graded rows** (every one except Sale's sinker), and
  spin axis in 6 of the 9 (Gaussian fits). These are the features most often implicated, but
  with several features flagged at once, KS alone cannot say which one the classifier uses.
- **The mixture barely moves anything.** BIC chose 2 components for Sale's changeup and
  fastball and for Misiorowski's fastball and slider, and 1 component everywhere else. AUC
  changed by 0.001 to 0.030 between the two models in those rows. The failures are not a "one
  blob is too simple" problem.
- **The one pass (Sale's sinker) is a small-sample pass.** Its interval, 0.406 to 0.663,
  includes values well above the bar. It is also the only graded row with no KS-flagged feature.
  Read it as "not shown to differ", not "matches".
- **8-feature diagnostic:** adding the two release angles changes AUC by at most about 0.05
  (largest: Misiorowski curveball, 0.812 to 0.760), so the angles are not what drives the
  failures.

### A likely contributor I did not fix (separate check, not from the CSVs)

Statcast reports most of these numbers rounded, and the generator produces continuous values. In
a quick check of Misiorowski's four-seamers in the raw CSV, `release_extension` takes only 14
distinct values over 1,677 pitches (0.1 ft steps) and `spin_axis` 27 distinct values (whole
degrees), while `release_spin_rate` has 403 and `release_pos_x` 88. A gradient-boosted
classifier can pick up "this extension is a multiple of 0.1" given enough samples, which would
explain why extension is flagged almost everywhere and why AUC climbs toward 1.0 as n_test
grows. The extension histogram for Misiorowski's fastball shows a comb pattern consistent with
this (visual observation, `results/m2/misiorowski-jacob/FF.png`). I have **not** tested this
(for instance by rounding generated values the same way and rerunning), so it is a hypothesis.
If it holds, part of the failure is a measurement-resolution artifact, not a modelling error,
and the test should be re-run after matching the rounding. That is a decision for Ryan; I
changed nothing.

### What the context table says

- **Context helped in 9 of 14 rows, but modestly.** The biggest gains are the fastballs:
  Ohtani +0.366 and Misiorowski +0.236 (log-likelihood per pitch). Sale's fastball is +0.048
  and his slider +0.073. Ohtani's slider (+0.001) is a tie.
- **This test doesn't say which context feature matters.** It uses all four variables
  together.
- **It hurt in 5 rows:** Sale's changeup (-0.212) and sinker (-0.072), Misiorowski's cutter
  (-0.013), Ohtani's cutter (-0.043), and **Ohtani's sinker (-8.020)**. The sinker row has only
  48 training and 5 held-out pitches, and the per-pitch log-likelihoods of -54 and -62 look like
  an ill-conditioned fit on a tiny set rather than a real effect (my reading). Ignore it.
- A gain here only says the conditioned model fits held-out numbers better. It was not run
  through the classifier test (by design: it is not gated on the earlier models passing).

## Movement (ungraded, M3's job)

Each PNG has a "movement, not graded until M3" panel with three clouds: real pitches from
Statcast's own fit, real held-out pitches flown by M1, and generated pitches flown by M1. No
number is reported for it. Visual observation only, for two plots:

- **Misiorowski's four-seamer** (`results/m2/misiorowski-jacob/FF.png`): the generated cloud
  sits on top of the real M1-flown cloud and is tighter than the real Statcast cloud, which
  spreads wider and extends to smaller horizontal break (toward -6 in) than either M1 cloud.
  That gap is M1's job to close with M3, not the generator's.
- **Sale's sinker** (`results/m2/sale-chris/SI.png`): the generated and M1-flown real clouds
  overlap around 17 to 19 in horizontal and 6 to 9 in vertical break, while the real Statcast
  points run lower and further out (down to about 0 in vertical). It looks like the
  seam-shifted-wake gap M1 flagged for sinkers. One plot, so not a general statement.

## What's still wrong

1. **The test mostly fails.** 8 of 9 graded rows are above the 0.55 bar, and all 8 are above 0.75.
   The milestone's done-condition (AUC close to 0.5) is not met.
2. **Possible quantization artifact** (see above): untested. It could turn small differences
   into near-perfect classification.
3. **Held-out games may differ from training games.** Fastball extension, spin rate and spin
   axis are KS-flagged for Sale and Misiorowski, which could reflect game-to-game drift (mound,
   tracking calibration) that a fit pooled over training games cannot predict. Not
   investigated.
4. **Many Ohtani pitch types are too small to grade.** He threw 14 games in 2025, so most of
   his rows have 5 to 27 held-out pitches.
5. **No numbers for movement.** The generated pitches are flown, but the comparison waits for M3.
6. **The context model is only scored by log-likelihood.** It is not used for sampling in these
   runs and not tested by the classifier.
7. **Nothing is saved.** Models are refit on every run; M7 will need them stored.

## Future work

- **Match the data's rounding** in the classifier test (or round the generated values) and
  re-run; decide whether the failures are real.
- **Pool across pitchers for scale.** One pitcher's pitch type has a few hundred pitches. A
  model with structure shared across pitchers (for example, a mean shift per pitcher) would
  have far more data. Out of scope for M2 by design.
- **Save fitted models** so M7 can load them.
- **M3 seam effect** to close the movement gap seen in the PNGs.
- **Pick context features per pitch type** instead of all four at once.
