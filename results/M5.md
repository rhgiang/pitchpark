# M5 results: a moving pitcher, rebuilt from broadcast video

**Short version:** the viewer now has a 3D Shohei Ohtani who winds up, lifts his leg, strides and
throws like the real one, with the ball leaving his hand exactly where Statcast says it does. His
motion comes from 19 broadcast clips of his bases-empty pitches (13 train, 6 held-out test, 3 games
in the test set). Ryan's eye test of the replica against the real clips: "it looks really good so you
have my okay for next steps".

**M5's "done when" is not met yet.** CLAUDE.md asks for recovered release point and arm slot within a
stated error of Statcast's *for several pitchers*. This milestone did one pitcher. The pipeline takes a
pitcher (clips directory + his Statcast CSV), so the follow-up is to run it on more pitchers. The
Ohtani numbers below are the stated error for him, and they are not good on extension (see "What's still
wrong").

All numbers are Ohtani, 2026, measured on the held-out test clips (6 clips from 3 games) unless a row
says otherwise. With only 6 clips the intervals are wide; they are 95% intervals from resampling games
(1000 draws, seed 0), and with 3 games they can only be read as a rough range.

## What was built

**Pipeline (Python, `src/pitchsim/pose/`, `scripts/m5_*.py`).** Each stage is one script, each pure
numpy library function is unit-tested on synthetic data (labelled synthetic):

1. Clips: Ryan saved 40 broadcast clips in his browser (see "Spike and clips").
2. 2D pose (RTMPose via `rtmlib`), screen for the centre-field view, find the pitcher.
3. Hand labels (release frame, foot-strike frame) from contact sheets.
4. 3D lift (HMR 2.0, in the separate `.venv-pose`), one body per frame.
5. Build: anchor each clip to Statcast feet and the field, average the clips, lock the feet, correct
   the stride, export `web/public/data/deliveries/<slug>.json` and the metrics.
6. 2D comparison of the replica to each clip.

**Viewer features added in this milestone (`web/`).**
- The animated figure, with a hand-built mitt, gripped ball, torso and clothes, and gaze toward the
  target (head 3 deg off at release). Frame rate: 8 draw calls, 10.6k triangles.
- A TV view (key 6): a high centre-field telephoto, calibrated from two real frames (camera
  ~[-22, 410, 32] ft, vertical field of view 2.6 deg), with a per-view near plane so the far figure
  does not flicker.
- A start screen: pick the pitcher from cards (throws, height, pitch mix, "full delivery" badge on
  Ohtani) before the fly-in. Skipped when the URL has `?pitcher=`.
- "Pitch Park": the stadium is named and carries original branding (no real team or sponsor marks).
- Ride camera that now follows the windup from behind, then glides to the over-the-shoulder release
  view.
- Frame pacing: idle 20 fps, playing capped at 60, DPR capped at 1.5, redraw on demand. At 2560x1440
  the GPU went from drawing every refresh to ~9% idle and ~27% while playing (at DPR 1, 144 Hz).
- The eye-test page `web/compare.html`: a local clip (file picker; never served or committed) beside
  the replica from the TV camera, synced on the release frame, with the projected replica skeleton
  overlaid from the fitted camera.

**Lifter spike (why HMR 2.0).** Hardware: RTX 5060 Ti. GVHMR (world-grounded, the better option) was
blocked: its checkpoints sit only on Google Drive and every download hit the daily quota; I did not use
unofficial mirrors. WHAM needs DPVO and mmcv compiled on Windows and was judged not worth a port. HMR 2.0
(4D-Humans, per frame) works: ~18 s per clip, with a few fixes for torch 2.11 / Python 3.12 / Windows (all
recreated by `scripts/setup_pose_env.ps1` and a committed patch). Cost of the choice: HMR 2.0 has no
world frame, so feet, scale and depth are added later (below). Rest-pose height reads 1.72 to 1.76 m for a
1.93 m pitcher (monocular scale is unobservable; the build rescales to his StatsAPI height, 76 in).
GVHMR is a parked optional upgrade. Licences: HMR 2.0 code is MIT, its checkpoint is non-commercial
research use, and the SMPL body model needs its own registration (see the README). Torch stays out of the
main environment.

## Detectors against hand labels (in-sample, flagged)

- **Release frame.** The detector takes the first speed peak of the hand at least half the clip's
  maximum (the pitch is the first big arm action). Against my 20 hand labels: median error 0 frames,
  max 53, 18 of 20 within 2 frames. This is IN-SAMPLE: the "first peak" rule was chosen after seeing
  those labels (two failures are early glitch peaks). A fresh check has n = 1 (error 2 frames), which is
  effectively no check. The build uses the hand label wherever one exists (every used clip has one); the
  detector only matters for future pitchers.
- **Release POSE = label minus 1 frame.** The label rule is "first frame the ball is visibly out", which
  lands at or after release; the hand moves ~2 ft per frame at 60 fps. At the label frame the per-clip 3D
  arm angle was 21.6 deg off Statcast, at label-1 8.0 deg, at label-2 1.8 deg. I took -1 because it is the
  last frame with the ball in the hand, not the error minimiser (-2 was lower). **This choice was made
  looking at the per-clip arm-angle error over all 20 labelled clips, test clips included**, so the
  arm-angle number is mildly selected on test data. The same goes for `MAX_UP_TILT_DEG = 15` (picked
  from "the 20 good clips" including test ones) and the foot-strike tolerance 0.13 torso lengths (picked
  on 12 labels).
- **Foot strike.** Taken from the 2D image (the lead ankle stops moving), because the 3D lift compresses
  depth and put foot strike 0.45 s before release, which was the "pause" Ryan saw. Against 12 hand labels
  (in-sample, tolerance fitted on them): median error 3 frames, max 5, within 2 frames 5 of 12; the
  detector fires 2 to 3 frames early (near heel contact; the labels mark foot flat). The build uses my
  labels where they exist (12 clips) and the detector otherwise (9, some of which are train clips only).

## Held-out release values, before the correction

Test clips n = 6, from 3 games. Statcast values are the per-pitch release for that exact pitch.

| Value | Own lift, median abs error [95% interval] | Averaged delivery vs the test pitches | Averaging cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Release x | 0.69 ft [0.41, 1.54] | 0.51 ft [0.45, 1.00] | -0.17 ft (averaging helps) |
| Release z | 0.65 ft [0.52, 1.09] | 0.75 ft [0.72, 0.89] | +0.10 ft |
| Extension | 3.67 ft [3.46, 3.99] | 3.37 ft [3.32, 3.52] | -0.30 ft |
| Arm angle | 7.4 deg [7.1, 8.4] | 8.2 deg [7.5, 10.0] | +0.8 deg |

"Own lift" is one clip's own lifted body against its own pitch. "Averaged" is the single averaged
delivery against each test pitch. The averaging cost is the difference: small, within the intervals, and
it can be negative, which is the point of averaging: the pitcher-to-pitcher scatter a single clip carries
(clip noise) goes away while the real mean remains. Every signed error is negative for x, extension
and arm angle and positive for z: a consistent bias, not noise (the body is too upright and too short in
stride from the front-on view). Adding the one screened-out test clip back widens the intervals a lot
(x up to 1.9 ft, arm up to 16 deg): the screen is doing real work.

## The correction: what is constructed, what is recovered

The averaged delivery is then moved to Statcast's training-games mean for bases-empty pitches (target
x -2.08 ft, z 5.66 ft, extension 6.59 ft, arm angle 33.4 deg; the target uses TRAIN games only, so the test
clips do not pull the delivery they are scored on). Before -> after:

| Value | Before | After | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Release x | -2.84 ft | -2.08 ft | constructed (partly unknowable: where he stands on the rubber) |
| Release z | 6.46 ft | 5.66 ft | constructed |
| Extension | 3.08 ft | 6.59 ft | **constructed**: the camera cannot measure it |
| Arm angle | 25.0 deg | 33.5 deg | constructed (arm turned 19.9 deg) |

How: a bone-length-preserving stride correction. The upper body moves 3.08 ft toward the plate and
0.62 ft over, the trunk bends 12.4 deg, and the legs and arm are re-solved with two-bone IK at fixed
bone lengths; bone-length change from the correction is 0.0000 mm (the foot lock adds 14.2 mm). Feet stay
planted: 0 in drift and 0 in height error for both toes (pivot until foot strike, lead afterwards). The
pelvis drops at most 0.95 ft (0.65 ft at release). The resulting stride (pivot toe to planted lead toe,
horizontal) is 5.21 ft: **constructed to match Statcast's mean extension; this camera did not measure it**.
Shape of the motion (timing, rocker step 1.23 ft at 2.62 s before release, leg lift, trunk rotation, arm
action) is recovered from the clips. The first approach (stretching the body 2.7x along the delivery)
distorted every segment, so it was dropped.

## The 2D comparison (replica against the real clip)

Per clip, one camera (an affine map from 3D to 2D, plus a per-frame 2D shift for pan) is fitted from the
replica's joints to the clip's RTMPose keypoints, matched at the release frame. Error is in torso lengths
(torso 21.9 in), median over joints and frames. Test clips n = 6, 3 games.

| Phase | Replica | Noise floor, 2D (other pitches' clips) | Noise floor, lifts (other pitches) | Own lift | Static release pose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All phases | **0.095** (2.1 in) | 0.072 | 0.105 | 0.065 | 0.426 |
| Rocker / set | 0.086 | 0.054 | 0.097 | 0.051 | 0.380 |
| Leg lift | 0.059 | 0.075 | 0.093 | 0.064 | 0.414 |
| Stride | 0.092 | 0.066 | 0.092 | 0.067 | 0.510 |
| Arm action | 0.142 | 0.082 | 0.127 | 0.105 | 0.315 |
| Follow-through | 0.173 | 0.134 | 0.146 | 0.089 | 0.533 |

Overall 95% intervals (games resampled): replica 0.086 to 0.108; 2D floor 0.068 to 0.073; lift floor
0.102 to 0.112; static pose 0.422 to 0.435. The "floor" is how far one real delivery is from another
real delivery by the same pitcher; a replica below it would be indistinguishable. Read-out: the
replica is about 4.5x closer than a frozen pose, about as close as the lifts of two different real
pitches are to each other (0.105), and still a bit above the 2D floor (0.072), mostly in arm action
and follow-through. On the 13 train clips the overall median is 0.088 [0.088, 0.106].

## Look-review history (Ryan, 2026-11-14)

- "Overall the movement looks super clunky ... the leg lands way before the actual throw". Cause: 3D
  foot strike 0.45 s before release (depth compression); fixed with 2D foot strike (now 0.167 s), a smooth
  PCHIP time warp instead of piecewise-linear, and Catmull-Rom interpolation in the viewer.
- The rocker step (Ohtani's side step before the leg lift) was added; the window grew to 2.85 s.
- "A slight pause with the leg lift that looks glitchy": 5 of the clips had bad lifts (2 leg swaps,
  3 frozen-then-snap frames). Repaired; leg-lift acceleration spikes fell from 185 to 222 ft/s^2 to 60 to
  125 ft/s^2.
- "Jittery, wobbly and bouncing": viewer pelvis bob 0.180 -> 0.107 ft, the release bounce removed, rig
  twist stabilised; a whole-body kink at t = -0.28 s cut 1.15 -> 0.45 in; windup wobble cut with
  phase-adaptive smoothing (shoulder-line wobble 78 -> 33 deg/s).
- Gaze: the head looked down; now it aims at the catcher's target (3 deg off at release; at leg lift
  it is 22 to 25 deg short at the neck limit).
- TV view "doesn't look like the actual broadcast": recalibrated from two real frames; "optimize the
  performance so it doesn't kill my gpu": frame pacing above.

**Ryan's verdicts, in his words.** On the motion: "the animation looks actually insanely good and so
accurate"; later "looks amazing"; asked for a line-up against the clips and a head that looks at the
batter (both done). On the eye-test page (2026-11-15): "it looks really good so you have my okay for next
steps".

## What's still wrong

- **Extension is not recovered.** From a front-on centre-field camera the stride runs along the line of
  sight and the lifter compresses depth: per clip 1.6 to 4.1 ft, median ~3.1 ft before correction,
  against Statcast ~6.5 ft. The final stride and extension are constructed from Statcast, not seen.
  Any extension-related claim for this replica is just Statcast's mean.
- **Arm action and follow-through are the weakest phases** (0.142 and 0.173 torso lengths, vs 0.082 and
  0.134 for the 2D floor): fast, motion-blurred, partly occluded.
- **The 3D figure sat lower than the video in the stride and follow-through.** Fixed on 2026-11-15 (see
  "Viewer fix: the arm in the dirt" below): the viewer now lowers the pelvis only for planted feet and
  follows the delivery's height, about 0.2-0.5 ft below it near release (the rig's legs are shorter).
- **Six test clips from 3 games**; 160 of the 200 planned clips were never downloaded (hand saving).
  Intervals are wide and the hold-out is narrow.
- **In-sample choices**: release-frame rule, release pose offset, tilt limit, foot-strike tolerance (see
  above).
- **Windup before every pitch:** the windup plays for runners-on pitches too, although the stretch
  delivery is a non-goal for M5.
- **Back leg** trails straight at release (concern raised at the look review, accepted).
- Body is 24 SMPL joints: no fingers and no gaze data; the hand and head are placed by rules.
- Parked minors from the final review: the start-screen menu downloads every pitcher's full export each
  time it opens (the fix is height, mix and delivery flag in `index.json`); the HMR 2.0 checkpoint
  download is not checksum-verified and torch's weights-only guard is off for it; repeated SMPL joint
  index tables and a repeated smootherstep helper could be one constants block; a shard run of the 2D
  step overwrites `pose2d.csv`; type hints missing on a few helpers (`mound_z_ft`, `fit_camera`,
  `parse_clip`).
- The web framing tests now exempt wild pitches: a path end wider than 2 ft (batter's eye) or 3 ft
  (umpire's view) from mid-plate, or higher than 5 ft, may leave the fixed cameras' frame (the cameras
  are Ryan's presets, not re-aimed): 251 batter-camera and 92 umpire-camera path ends of 6,035 pitches
  (all 9 exported pitcher files) were exempt, mostly wild pitches and no-spin counterfactuals. Release
  and zone corners must always fit, and the exemption is capped at 5% of pitches.

## Follow-up for M5's "done when"

Run the same pipeline on more pitchers. It needs, per pitcher: his clips in `data/video/<slug>/`
(saved by hand), `m5_pose2d.py`, hand labels of the release frame (and optionally foot strike) in
`src/pitchsim/pose/labels/<slug>_*.csv`, the lift, then `m5_build.py <clips-dir> --raw <csv>` and
`m5_compare2d.py`. With 3 to 4 pitchers and the same error table, the "several pitchers" criterion can be
stated, and the extension question can be settled (a second camera angle or world-grounded lifting such
as GVHMR is the likely route).

### Fixes made along the way (each with a synthetic, labelled test)

- **Left-handers (first on real data).** The foot-strike contact sheets and the foot-strike score in
  `m5_label_frames.py` used the left ankle and "R" for every pitcher. They now use the glove-side ankle
  (`release.lead_ankle_idx`). Two mirror tests cover the rest. A left-handed delivery, mirrored from the
  right-handed one, comes out of `correct_to_mean` as the exact mirror of the right-handed result. `anchor`
  puts a LHP's left toe on the rubber. Everything else was already handedness-aware: pivot and lead toes,
  throwing hand, arm-angle sign, release x, gaze and the viewer's figure. The screenshots show Sale throwing
  left-handed, with the glove on his right hand, landing on his right foot.
- **Low arm slots.** The screen required the wrist to rise 0.45 torso lengths above the shoulder. That bar was
  tuned on Ohtani (~33 deg arm angle) and rejected real Sale clips: his wrist peaks at ~0.38. The bar now
  scales with sin(arm angle) of the pitcher's train pitches and never goes above 0.45, so Ohtani's screen
  is unchanged. Three clips the lower bar let through were the catcher's throw back to the mound; they are in
  the hand-review list.
- **No rocker step.** Neither Misiorowski nor Sale takes one. The rocker knot is now used only when at least
  half the train clips show a step (`delivery.has_rocker_step`). Otherwise there is no knot and no windup
  fade. Before this fix, the build took the median of an empty list.
- **Back-knee snap.** In both builds, the averaged lift's pelvis drifted ~1 ft back toward the rubber just
  before the late foot strike, then rushed forward. The corrected back knee then snapped 41 deg in one frame
  (Misiorowski). `correct_to_mean` now moves the pelvis only toward the plate from leg lift on (a running
  minimum, smoothed). Results for Misiorowski:
  - the back knee's largest change fell to 15.5 deg/frame;
  - the final jerk fell from 4,522 to 3,470.

  Ohtani's averaged lift drifts back ~0.5 ft at the same point. His published delivery was NOT rebuilt (Ryan
  signed it off); rebuilding him would apply this fix and change his stride slightly.

- The clips are still hand-saved broadcasts with many tight shots: only 13 to 21 of 40 were usable.

### Viewer fix: the arm in the dirt (2026-11-15)

## Sale arm path: 2D-guided arm refinement (2026-11-16)

Ryan: Sale's very horizontal arm path is "somewhat good but not completely accurate". The release arm angle
was already close (1.6 deg per held-out clip); the path was not: his arm-action score was 2x the 2D floor and
the throwing upper arm collapsed (1.04 -> 0.28 ft, ~150 deg in two frames) as it pointed at the centre-field
camera. Ohtani (0.35 ft) and Misiorowski (0.33 ft) collapse too; it was just largest for Sale.

**What changed** (`pitchsim/pose/arm_refine.py`, default on in `m5_build.py`, `--no-arm-refine` skips it):
for the window foot strike - 0.2 s .. release + 0.2 s (full effect from fs - 0.1 s to release + 0.1 s, smootherstep
edges) the throwing elbow, wrist and hand are re-solved so their projections match the TRAIN clips' 2D keypoints.
- Fixed segment lengths: the standing values (median over the set frames: 1.04 / 0.98 / 0.34 ft for Sale),
  not the release frame's (the forearm is depth-compressed there: 0.70 ft).
- Per train clip, the camera is the affine fit of the 2D comparison, through the build's time alignment (PCHIP
  through foot strike and release). Targets are the clip's elbow and wrist positions (confidence >= 0.5).
- Depth sign: the four depth-flip combinations of the two bone directions per frame, Viterbi over frames with a
  turn cost, a prior toward the current lift (trusted only where the bone is near full length) and a hinge-reversal
  (hyperextension) penalty. Then a joint least squares: 2D data + angular acceleration + jerk of the joint paths +
  the hand staying on its corrected path + the hand pinned at the release frame.
- **After `correct_to_mean`, not before.** The correction rotates the arm by up to ~20 deg in the x-z plane, which is the
  image plane the 2D data constrains; a refinement before it would be undone there. The release hand is pinned,
  so release values stay on target (checked in the build: x/z/extension within 0.005 ft, arm angle within 0.05 deg).
- Tests (synthetic, labelled): a made-up arm whose depth collapses mid-swing gets its lengths and path back; a
  well-conditioned arm is left alone; no tracks means no change. `blendDirection` (viewer) is tested too.
