# M3 results: the push spin can't explain, on five real pitchers

**Short version:** adding a learned constant push to M1's flight cuts the median held-out plate
miss in every pitch type, and **16 of 17 graded pitch types pass** the rule Ryan set (a drop of at
least 0.6 in, with its 95% interval above zero), under M1's current 0.20 lower bound on spin
efficiency. **With that bound lowered, 14 of 17 pass** (see "The 0.20 efficiency bound"). The one
fail as reported is Misiorowski's four-seamer (drop 0.38 in). Graded medians go from 2.03–8.26 in (M1 only) to 1.49–3.15 in. The sinkers,
changeups and sweepers that M1 missed in one consistent direction lose almost all of their
bias, which is what `CLAUDE.md` asked of M3.

Three things limit what that means:

- **The data mostly shows a fixed habit, not a per-pitch effect.** A single constant push per
  pitch type gets a median 88% of the drop. The per-pitch model beats it with an interval above
  zero in 9 of 17 graded rows (7 of 17 with the bound lowered), usually by a few tenths of an inch.
  The largest per-pitch wins are mostly the efficiency bound, not a release-driven push. This goes
  against the goal of a per-pitch push.
- **This does not show the push is seam force.** It is "whatever closes the miss left after M1".
  It holds a release-state artefact, M1's efficiency absorbs a push on the spin-force line as far as
  efficiency can move inside its 0.2–1.0 bounds, and four-seamers need a push about as large as
  many other types. Three of the four
  graded four-seamers pass.
- **The stretch target is only partly met.** 10 of 17 graded rows are at or under 2 in; none is
  at or under 1 in.

Real results below come from `results/m3/<pitcher>/summary.csv`, `knock_on.csv` and `skipped.txt`,
and `results/m3/bound_check.csv` (`scripts/seam_bound_check.py`), with the tables generated from
those files, not typed. Pitch and game counts come from `train_seam.py`'s printed output and the
cached CSVs. Synthetic numbers are in
their own section and come from `results/synthetic/m3_controls.csv`
(`scripts/seam_controls.py`). Numbers from a one-off check are labelled as such.

## What was measured

- **Data:** Statcast, regular season. Ohtani 2025 (753 usable pitches, 14 games), Sale 2026
  (2,544 of 2,575, 27 games), Misiorowski 2026 (2,647 of 2,649, 30 games), and two pitchers new
  to this milestone: Sánchez 2026 (3,158, 33 games) and King 2026 (2,981, 32 games).
  Sale and Sánchez are left-handed, the others right-handed.
- **Split by game:** 70% of each pitcher's games for fitting, 30% held out, seed 0, same split as
  M1 and M2. Held-out games: Ohtani 4, Sale 8, Misiorowski 9, Sánchez 9, King 9.
- **Three variants, same held-out pitches:** **M1** (calibrated Cd and spin efficiency, no push),
  **fixed** (M1 plus one constant (x, z) acceleration per pitch type, the training mean), and
  **per-pitch** (M1 plus a ridge regression that predicts the push from what's known at the hand:
  spin axis as sin/cos, spin rate, speed, release x/z, extension). A pitch's own movement is never
  an input.
- **The push being learned:** per training pitch, the constant acceleration (ft/s²) that makes
  M1's flight land on the Statcast fit crossing at y = 17/12 ft. It acts inside the equations of
  motion, not as a shift at the plate.
- **Miss:** distance in the x–z plane between simulated and fit crossing, in inches, from the
  release point with per-game air density, as in M1.
- **Drop:** M1's median miss minus per-pitch's median miss. **`pp_vs_fixed`:** fixed's median minus
  per-pitch's median; above zero means the per-pitch slopes helped. Both intervals are 95%,
  from resampling held-out **games** (1,000 draws), because pitches in a game share air and mound.

## The pass rule

Set by Ryan on 2026-11-04, after the no-seam control below and before these real results were
run.

- **Graded** means at least 30 held-out pitches and at least 2 held-out games.
- **Pass** means the drop is at least 0.6 in and the interval's lower end is above zero.

The two targets are goals, reported beside the rule, and don't decide a pass: the **realistic bar**
(as accurate as the same pitcher's four-seamer) and the **stretch** (1–2 in).

## Per-pitch or fixed habit?

**Mostly a fixed habit.** The rule from the spec: if the per-pitch model doesn't beat fixed on
held-out games, say the data shows a fixed habit.

- **8 of 17 graded rows show a fixed habit** (`pp_vs_fixed` interval reaches zero or below):
  Ohtani FF*, Sale FF, SI and SL, Misiorowski CU and FF, Sánchez CH, King CH.
- **9 of 17 show per-pitch beating fixed**, but three of those only just (lower end +0.00 for
  Sánchez SI and King FF, +0.03 for Sale CH), and the typical gain is small (median `pp_vs_fixed`
  across the 17 rows: 0.17 in).
- **Three rows gain more than 0.6 in as reported:** Misiorowski SL +1.89, King SL +1.82, King ST
  +0.76. **The two slider wins are mostly the efficiency bound, not a release-driven push.** With the
  bound lowered they fall to +0.71 and +0.20 (next section), and King's slider no longer beats fixed.
  Whatever remains for Misiorowski's slider may be the spin axis moving between clusters during the
  season (`results/M2.md`): two shapes or a drift rather than a pitch-to-pitch effect. I did not test
  that, or look at which input carries the slope.
- **Without the bound, per-pitch beats fixed in 7 of 17 graded rows, not 9.** King SL and Sánchez SL
  drop out. Two rows still gain more than 0.6 in: King ST and Misiorowski SL.
- **For changeups and sinkers a constant push does most of the work.** Changeups gain +0.02 to +0.17
  in and sinkers +0.11 to +0.36 in. Three of those six rows do beat fixed (Sale CH, Sánchez SI, King
  SI), but by small amounts.
- Per-pitch never does significantly worse than fixed in a graded row, so keeping it does no
  measured harm. It just mostly isn't earning its slopes.

## Synthetic controls (fake pitches, not results)

All from `make(n=1500, seed=0)`, constant air, known physics, **no measurement noise**. They are
labelled synthetic and exist only to show what the method does when the truth is known. Reproduce
with `python scripts/seam_controls.py`.

**1. With no seam force at all, M3 still "finds" a drop.** That is where the 0.6 in floor comes from.

| Synthetic type | Drop with no seam force (in) | `pp_vs_fixed` (in) |
|---|---|---|
| CH | 0.527 | 0.020 |
| CU | 0.369 | 0.028 |
| FF | 0.267 | 0.035 |
| SL | 0.595 | 0.004 |

The drop here is real improvement against the fit, but it has nothing to do with seams: the push
is just removing a constant offset M1 has. Three cautions on the floor:

- It is an estimate from noise-free fake pitches, one arsenal, one seed. Real data could differ.
- **SL sits at 0.595, just under 0.6, so the floor has almost no margin.** On a different
  seed a no-seam type could clear it.
- Those drops had tight intervals (widest 0.034 in), so the interval test alone would have passed
  all four. The 0.6 in floor is doing the work.

**2. The learned push includes a release-state artefact.** `release_state` runs Statcast's
constant-acceleration fit back to the release point, but a real (or simulated) ball isn't under
constant acceleration, so the start velocity comes out wrong: median absolute error
0.12 / 0.24 / 0.08 ft/s in x / y / z. With the **true** Cd and efficiency and no seam force, that
alone needs these pushes to close the miss:

| Synthetic type | Spurious push x, z (ft/s²) | Miss with true physics, no push (in) |
|---|---|---|
| CU | -0.37, +1.09 | 1.56 |
| SL | -0.49, +0.54 | 0.83 |
| CH | +0.56, +0.26 | 0.68 |
| FF | +0.46, -0.19 | 0.46 |

So up to about 1 ft/s² of a learned push can be artefact, and **push direction isn't pure seam
physics**. Real pushes run 1.60 to 7.59 ft/s² in graded rows. The large ones (sinkers, 5.6 to 7.6)
can't be mostly artefact on this estimate, but the small ones (Misiorowski FF and SL at 1.60, Sale
SL 2.13, Misiorowski CU 2.20) could be a large part artefact.

This also bears on the stretch target. About 0.6 in (median 0.645 over all types) is the synthetic
miss against the fit from a release start, even with perfect physics. A learned push can close it
on paper (the synthetic model reaches 0.003–0.014 in), but only by fitting the artefact. So a real
median well under 1 in would partly be the model matching Statcast's curve fit, not the ball.

**3. M1's efficiency absorbs a push on the spin-force line, as far as efficiency can move inside
its 0.2–1.0 bounds.** A constant push on that line looks like more or less spin, so calibration
soaks it up by changing efficiency. Past a bound it can't, and the rest shows as a miss. The
synthetic CH's spin-force direction is (-0.86, +0.51) in x, z. `cos` is the planted push's cosine
with it: -1 opposes the force, +1 goes with it, 0 is square to it.

| Planted CH push (ft/s²) | cos | CH efficiency | M1 miss (in) | Per-pitch miss (in) | `pp_vs_fixed` (in) | Fitted push x, z | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| None | | 0.815 | 0.53 | 0.003 | +0.02 | +0.24, +0.41 | no |
| (5, -4), against the spin force | -0.99 | **0.383** | **0.56** | 0.017 | **+0.43** | -0.13, -0.34 | no |
| (-5, +4), with the spin force | +0.99 | **1.000 (bound)** | **4.99** | 0.006 | +0.15 | -3.17, +3.27 | yes |
| (10, -8), against, doubled | -0.99 | **0.200 (bound)** | **2.90** | 0.034 | +0.72 | +1.32, -2.08 | yes |
| (4, 5), off the spin force | -0.14 | 0.724 | **6.98** | 0.007 | +0.06 | +3.28, +5.52 | yes |

The (5, -4) push opposes the spin force. It moved efficiency from 0.815 to 0.383 and left M1's
miss about where it was, so that type fails the pass rule. **So a small residual doesn't prove a
pitch has no seam effect**, as long as efficiency had room to move, and this is a reason not to read
Misiorowski's fastball fail as "no seam effect". The same push in the other sense, or a doubled one,
hits a bound and is plainly visible. So on the six real rows at 0.20, part of what M1 would
otherwise absorb shows up instead.

An absorbed push isn't quite invisible either. In the (5, -4) row the fixed model leaves 0.449 in
and per-pitch 0.017 in (`pp_vs_fixed` +0.43, against +0.02 with no push): a constant push wrongly
booked as spin scales with each pitch's spin, so the per-pitch slopes pick it up. That is the same
trace seen on the real bound rows. The off-axis row shows a visible push isn't recovered exactly
either: (+3.28, +5.52) came back for a planted (4, 5), because efficiency absorbed part of that too.

## What's still wrong

1. **The per-pitch goal isn't supported for most pitch types.** The extra push looks mostly like a
   fixed habit. The largest per-pitch wins are mostly the 0.20 efficiency bound, not a
   release-driven push: without it, 14 of 17 pass rather than 16 and per-pitch beats fixed in 7 of
   17 rather than 9. Only King's sweeper and Misiorowski's slider still gain more than 0.6 in, and
   neither cause is established.
2. **The push isn't identified as seam force.** It mixes seam effect (if any), release-state
   artefact, and every other M1 direction error. Four-seamers needing 1.6 to 4.6 ft/s² may mean M1's
   spin direction or lift is off generally, not just for seam pitches. Separating these would
   need a joint refit of efficiency and push, which is a spec non-goal.
3. **Part of any seam push can hide in M1's efficiency**, as far as efficiency can move inside its
   bounds. Fitted efficiencies stay low (four-seamers 0.48–0.72) and six rows sit on the 0.20 bound.
   Whether to change that bound is Ryan's decision.
4. **The 0.6 in floor has almost no margin** and comes from noise-free synthetic data.
5. **1 in isn't reached.** 1.49 in is the best graded median, and seven graded rows are above 2 in.
   Misiorowski's curveball (3.15 in) and King's sweeper (2.95 in) are the worst.
6. **Ohtani can't really be judged.** Four held-out games, and five of six fitted rows ungraded,
   including the sweeper and sinker that motivated M3.
7. **Missing venue for King.** One training game (825094, 106 pitches) was at Estadio Alfredo Harp
   Helú in Mexico City, which isn't in `VENUE_ELEVATION_FT`, so it was flown at 0 ft
   (the documented fallback). The stadium is high up: I tried about 7,300 ft, a figure from memory
   and not checked. That one-off check (**not produced by any script in `scripts/`**) left all five
   King rows passing, and moved medians by at most 0.13 in (drops by at most 0.14 in, the slider's
   `pp_vs_fixed` by 0.23 in). Some counts change: King's slider goes
   from 1.98 to 2.08 in (over the 2 in line), and King's fastball `pp_vs_fixed` lower end goes from
   +0.004 to -0.041 (it would join the fixed-habit list). In counts: at or under 2 in goes from 10
   to 9 of 17, and per-pitch beating fixed from 9 to 8 of 17. The harder four-seam bar goes from 4
   of 10 to 2 or 3 of 10, because King's four-seamer moves to 1.96 in, so his slider misses it and
   his changeup (1.963 vs 1.962) is a tie at best. The tables above use the 0 ft run.
8. **No season drift.** Slopes are fitted across a season in which some pitches moved
   (`results/M2.md`).
9. **Nothing is saved.** Models are refit on each run; M7 will need them stored.

## Future work

- **Add the Mexico City venue** with a checked elevation, and rerun King.
- **Joint refit of efficiency and push**, to see how much of a seam push M1 is hiding.
- **Start from a better release state** (fit the flight rather than extrapolating), which would
  remove the artefact and let the floor be re-derived.
- **Repeat the no-seam control over several seeds and with noise**, to give the floor some margin.
